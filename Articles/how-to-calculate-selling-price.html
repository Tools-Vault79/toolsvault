How to Calculate Selling Price: Formula, Examples & Pricing Guide

Learn how to calculate selling price using total cost and desired profit margin, with simple formulas and practical examples.

ToolVault  ›  Articles  ›  How to Calculate Selling Price

Setting the right selling price is important for any business that sells products or services. A price that is too low may leave little room for profit, while a price that is too high can make a product difficult to sell.

The calculation can be straightforward when you know your total cost and desired profit margin. This guide explains the selling price formula, provides examples and explains the difference between markup and profit margin.

What Is Selling Price?

The selling price is the amount a customer pays for a product or service.

It is different from your cost. Your cost represents what the business spends to produce or acquire the product, while the selling price is the amount charged to the customer.

The difference between selling price and total cost contributes to the business's profit, although other business expenses may also affect the final profit.

Selling Price Formula

When you know your total cost and desired profit margin, a useful formula is:

Selling Price = Total Cost ÷ (1 − Profit Margin)

The profit margin should be expressed as a decimal. For example, a 25% profit margin is written as 0.25.

If your total cost is $40 and your desired profit margin is 25%:

$40 ÷ (1 − 0.25) = $53.33

The calculated selling price would therefore be approximately $53.33.

How to Calculate Selling Price Step by Step

Step 1 — Calculate Your Total Cost

First determine how much the product actually costs your business.

Depending on the product, this may include:

  • Purchase or production cost
  • Packaging
  • Shipping
  • Transaction fees
  • Direct labor
  • Other directly related expenses

For a more accurate calculation, include the relevant costs before deciding on the final price.

Step 2 — Determine Your Desired Profit Margin

Next, decide what profit margin you want to achieve.

  • 10% margin = 0.10
  • 20% margin = 0.20
  • 25% margin = 0.25
  • 30% margin = 0.30

Your target margin directly affects the required selling price.

Step 3 — Calculate the Selling Price

Apply the selling price formula:

Selling Price = Total Cost ÷ (1 − Profit Margin)

For example, suppose your total cost is $60 and you want a 30% margin:

$60 ÷ (1 − 0.30) = $85.71

The calculated selling price is approximately $85.71.

Selling Price Example

Imagine a small business sells a product with the following costs:

Cost Amount
Product cost $30
Packaging $5
Other direct costs $5
Total cost $40

The business wants a 25% profit margin.

$40 ÷ (1 − 0.25) = $53.33

The calculated selling price is therefore approximately $53.33.

The business can then consider other factors such as competitors, customer demand, taxes, marketplace fees and overall pricing strategy before establishing the final customer price.

Selling Price vs Cost Price

Cost price is what the business pays to acquire or produce a product.

Selling price is what the customer pays.

For example:

Cost price: $40
Selling price: $53.33

The difference between these amounts is important when evaluating profitability. However, the difference alone does not necessarily represent final business profit because other expenses may still need to be considered.

Selling Price vs Markup

Markup and profit margin are related but they are not the same calculation.

Markup measures the increase over cost, while profit margin measures profit as a percentage of the selling price.

For example, if an item costs $40 and is sold for $50:

Markup:
$10 ÷ $40 = 25%

Profit margin:
$10 ÷ $50 = 20%

This is why businesses should be clear about whether they are targeting a markup percentage or a profit-margin percentage when calculating prices.

Common Pricing Mistakes

Ignoring Additional Costs

A business may calculate the price using only the purchase or production cost while overlooking packaging, shipping, transaction fees or other relevant expenses.

Confusing Markup With Margin

A 25% markup does not produce a 25% profit margin. The two percentages use different bases for calculation.

Copying Competitors Without Calculating Costs

Competitor prices can provide useful market context, but your own costs still need to be understood.

Setting a Price Without Considering the Customer

A mathematically calculated price is only a starting point. Market demand, positioning and customer expectations can also affect the final price.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the selling price formula?

For a target profit margin, the formula is:

Selling Price = Total Cost ÷ (1 − Profit Margin)

How do I calculate selling price from cost?

First calculate your total relevant cost. Then determine your desired profit margin and apply the selling-price formula.

What is the difference between markup and profit margin?

Markup compares profit with cost, while profit margin compares profit with selling price.

Can I calculate selling price online?

Conclusion

Calculating selling price starts with understanding your total cost and deciding what profit margin you want to achieve.

The basic formula is:

Selling Price = Total Cost ÷ (1 − Profit Margin)

The calculated result is a useful starting point, but businesses should also consider market conditions, competitors, customer expectations and other relevant expenses before establishing a final price.
