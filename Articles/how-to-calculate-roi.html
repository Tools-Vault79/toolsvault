Return on Investment, commonly called ROI, is a simple way to measure the gain or loss generated by an investment compared with its total cost.

Whether you are evaluating a marketing campaign, business project, product investment or another financial activity, calculating ROI can help you understand the return generated from the money invested.

In this guide, you'll learn the basic ROI formula, how to calculate ROI step by step, and how to use ToolVault's online ROI calculator.

What Is ROI?

ROI stands for Return on Investment.

It expresses the gain or loss from an investment as a percentage of the investment cost.

A positive ROI means the return is greater than the investment cost, while a negative ROI means the calculated return is lower than the investment cost.

ROI Formula

The basic ROI formula is:

ROI = (Net Gain ÷ Total Investment Cost) × 100

First calculate the net gain:

Net Gain = Total Return − Total Investment Cost

For example, suppose an investment has the following figures:

Initial investment: $1,000

Additional costs: $100

Total return: $1,500

The total investment cost is:

$1,000 + $100 = $1,100

The net gain is:

$1,500 − $1,100 = $400

Therefore:

($400 ÷ $1,100) × 100 = 36.36%

The calculated ROI in this example is approximately 36.36%.

How to Calculate ROI Step by Step

Step 1: Determine the Initial Investment

Start with the amount originally invested in the project, product, campaign or activity.

Step 2: Add Additional Costs

Include other costs that are relevant to the investment calculation.

Step 3: Calculate Total Investment Cost

Total Investment Cost = Initial Investment + Additional Costs

Step 4: Determine the Total Return

Enter the total amount generated or received from the investment.

Step 5: Calculate Net Gain

Net Gain = Total Return − Total Investment Cost

Step 6: Calculate ROI

ROI = (Net Gain ÷ Total Investment Cost) × 100

What Is a Good ROI?

There is no single ROI percentage that applies to every investment. ROI needs to be interpreted in context because results can vary depending on the investment type, time period, risk, industry and total costs involved.

An ROI percentage is therefore most useful when considered alongside the circumstances and objectives of the investment.

ROI vs Return Multiple

ROI and return multiple describe investment performance in different ways.

For example, if the total investment cost is $1,000 and the total return is $1,500:

Total investment cost: $1,000

Total return: $1,500

Net gain: $500

ROI: 50%

Return multiple: 1.50x

Common ROI Calculation Mistakes

1. Ignoring Additional Costs

Using only the initial investment can produce a different result when additional costs are part of the investment.

2. Confusing Return With Profit

Total return and net gain are different figures. Net gain is calculated after subtracting the total investment cost from the total return.

3. Forgetting the Time Period

ROI percentages should be considered alongside the period over which the return was generated.

4. Using Inconsistent Figures

Investment and return figures should represent comparable periods and relevant costs.
